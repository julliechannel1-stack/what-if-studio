You step out into your backyard tonight and look up, and the Moon is gone. Not hidden behind a cloud. Not dimmed by haze. Gone, like someone reached up and switched off the brightest lamp in the sky. The stars look sharper without it, and the dark feels deeper, and your own shadow has vanished from the grass at your feet. Your phone still says the night is clear, and your neighbour still waves from her porch, and the crickets still sing from the hedge, but the silver disc that has hung above every human night is simply not there. You blink, and you rub your eyes, and you wait for it to slide back into view, and it never does. The sky feels wrong in a way you cannot name yet, and your stomach knows it before your brain does. Something huge has left, and the night will never feel the same, and the changes have already started, right now, while you stand there staring up.
