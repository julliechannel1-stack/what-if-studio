When morning comes, the Sun rises on schedule, and for a moment you almost believe the night was a dream. Then you see the sea. At the harbour, boats rest on mud at midday, and fishermen mend nets they cannot use, and children poke at rock pools that the tide forgot to fill. The Moon pulled the oceans into two great bulges, one facing it and one on the far side, and those bulges chased the spinning Earth around the planet, giving every coast its rhythm of high water and low water. Without that pull, only the Sun is left to tug at the sea, and the Sun is far away, and its tides are small and weak. The water still moves, but the great rise and fall has shrunk to a shallow sigh, and the marshes and mudflats that fed millions of birds and fish begin to dry, and the coast starts to forget how to be a coast. The sea does not vanish, and the Sun does not stop shining, and yet the coast you love is already changing its shape,
