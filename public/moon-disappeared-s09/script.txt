Humans adapt, because that is what humans do, and the adaptation is both clever and sad. Cities switch on brighter lights to replace the moonlit streets, and sailors learn to navigate by stars and screens, and poets search for a new symbol for longing, because the old one has left the sky. Children born after the disappearance grow up hearing stories about a silver face that watched over their parents, and they point at old photographs and ask if it was real, the way you might ask about a legend. Festivals that once followed the full moon move to fixed dates on a wall, and the dates feel hollow, and the songs still mention moonlight, and the singers close their eyes when they reach that word. You stand under a sky full of stars, and the stars are beautiful, and they are not enough, and you understand at last that the Moon was never decoration.
