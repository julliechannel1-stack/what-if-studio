The Earth itself starts to change its rhythm, too, in ways that are quiet and strange. The Moon has been braking our spin for ages, dragging on the oceans and slowing each day by the tiniest fraction, like a hand trailing in water. Without that drag, the days stop stretching and settle into a new, slightly faster beat, and the winds and currents that the tides once stirred begin to move differently. Deep ocean water that used to mix and rise, carrying food to whales and fish, grows still and layered, and the great conveyor of the seas slows down. Coral reefs that timed their spawning to the full moon release their eggs into a dark sea with no signal, and the clouds of tiny life drift apart instead of meeting. You eat fish from a market stall and you never see the chain that broke, but the empty nets and the silent reefs tell the story, and the sea grows poorer, year by year, without its moon.
