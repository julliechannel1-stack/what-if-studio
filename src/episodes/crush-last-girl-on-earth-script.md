## Fact (verified 2026-11-20, re-checked this run)

Conservation biology's fifty / five hundred rule: an effective breeding population of
about fifty to avoid inbreeding in the short term, about five hundred to keep enough
genetic variety for generations. Two people cannot restart humanity — that is the turn.
Sources: https://biologyinsights.com/what-is-the-minimum-viable-population-for-humans/
and https://biologyinsights.com/how-many-people-would-it-take-to-repopulate-the-earth/
Nuance kept out of the narration but true: later work suggests the thresholds may be
higher (about one hundred / about one thousand), and "effective" breeders are fewer
than the head count — so "about fifty / about five hundred" is the classic rule of
thumb, worded as "scientists say" in the script, not as a precise census count.

## s01 — The empty morning

You wake up to a phone with no signal and a street with no sound. No traffic, no
voices, no music from the shop on the corner, just your own breathing in a room that
feels too big. You call out for your mother, and nobody answers. You run downstairs,
and the front door is standing open, and the whole street is empty. You walk to
school, and the gates are open, and the classrooms are empty, and the chairs are
pushed back like everyone left in the middle of a lesson. You shout her name into
the silence, and for a second you think you imagined the whole world. Then you turn
a corner, and there she is, standing in the middle of the road, holding her school
bag like it is the only normal thing left. Your crush. The only other person left
on Earth. And she is looking straight at you.

## s02 — One perfect day

For one perfect day the empty world is yours. You take whatever you want from the
silent shops, and you eat ice cream for dinner, and you ride a bus with no driver
all the way to the end of the line just because you can. She laughs like she never
laughed at school, loud and free, with nobody watching and nobody judging and
nobody standing between you and her. You race her down the middle of the road, and
you play music from an empty shop until the whole street shakes, and for the first
time ever she sits next to you without looking at her phone, without looking away,
without pretending you are not there. It feels like a dream you never want to wake
up from. It feels like the world ended just to give you this one day. And for a
while, you almost believe that is enough.

## s03 — The quiet starts to press

Then the quiet starts to press on you. A city with no voices is too big and too
still, and the nights are the worst, with no traffic and no neighbours and no one
else awake anywhere. You lie in bed and listen to nothing, and nothing answers
back, and you understand something you never knew before. People are not background
noise. People are the thing that makes a place feel alive. A street is just concrete
without footsteps. A school is just empty rooms without laughter. Even a perfect day
feels hollow when there is nobody to tell about it, nobody to miss you when you are
gone, nobody who would notice if you never came home. She feels it too. You can see
it in her eyes when the sun goes down. The world is beautiful, and it is empty, and
the two of you are alone in it.

## s04 — The turn

Here is the turn, and it changes everything you thought this story was about. Two
people cannot restart humanity. Scientists say about fifty breeding adults are needed
to avoid inbreeding in the short term, and about five hundred to keep enough variety
for generations. Fifty. Five hundred. And there are two of you. Just two. You and
she are not the beginning of a new world. You are the last page of the old one. The
question is no longer who likes who. The question is what survives. Every book, every
song, every story, every recipe, every name of every person who ever lived — all of
it is sitting in empty buildings right now, waiting for someone to save it. And the
only someone left is you. The only someone left is her. The crush was never the
whole story. It was only the beginning.

## s05 — Saving what matters

So you stop playing, and you start saving. You carry books out of the library, and
seeds out of the garden shop, and you learn to grow food, and you learn to fix a
generator, and you learn to keep the lights on. She keeps the lists, and you keep
the tools, and the two of you become a team, because knowledge is the only thing
that can outlive the people who made it. You save the music first, because a world
with no music is not a world worth rebuilding. You save the seeds next, because a
world with no food is not a world at all. And every night you sit together and write
down what you learned that day, so that if anyone ever comes after you, they will
know that two kids in an empty city refused to let the whole world disappear.

## s06 — The crush changes

And somewhere between the planting and the fixing, the crush changes into something
steadier. Liking someone from across a classroom costs nothing. Choosing the same
person every morning in an empty world costs everything. You choose her when the
generator breaks, and she chooses you when the food runs low, and you choose each
other again when the nights get long and the silence gets loud. When she takes your
hand, it is not a school crush any more. It is a promise. A promise that you will
not give up, that you will keep the lights on, that you will keep each other going
when there is nobody else left to do it. And you finally understand what she was
worth all along. Not a secret to keep. Not a dream to chase. A person to stand
beside, in a world that needs standing beside.

## s07 — The payoff

Here is the payoff the empty world leaves you with. You always wanted her all to
yourself, and now you have her, and it turns out the real gift was never being alone
with her. The real gift was becoming the kind of person she would choose to rebuild
with. You did not get the world you wanted. You got something harder, and something
better. A reason to learn, a reason to save, a reason to keep going when everything
is gone. And if the world ever comes back, it will come back because two people
refused to let it end quietly. So here is the question for you, and it is the only
one that matters now.

On-screen comment card, exact text:
If you were the last two people on Earth, what is the first thing you would save, and why?
