Over long years, that slow drift turns into something you can feel in your bones. The lean of the Earth sets your seasons, because when your half of the planet tips toward the Sun, you get long days and summer heat, and when it tips away, you get short days and winter cold. If the lean wanders, the seasons wander with it, and a place that grew wheat for a hundred summers may find its summers shorter and colder, while a frozen coast may find its winters melting into mud. Rain falls where drought used to rule, and drought grips valleys that used to bloom, and farmers plant by calendars that no longer match the sky. Nobody wakes up one morning to a new climate. It slides, season by season, harvest by harvest, year after year, until your grandchildren live in a world whose weather your grandparents would not recognize, all because a steadying hand left the spinning top alone.
