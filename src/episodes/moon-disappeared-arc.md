# What If the Moon Disappeared Tonight? — Arc

Slug: moon-disappeared

Verified fact (the turn): NASA, Facts About Earth — "It stabilizes our planet's wobble, which has made the climate less variable over thousands of years." Source: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/ — NASA also states Earth's axis is tilted about twenty-three point four degrees, and that tilt causes the yearly cycle of seasons.

Hook: You step into your backyard tonight, look up, and the Moon is simply gone, with no cloud and no haze to explain it, and the night feels wrong before you can name why.

Turn: The Moon is not decoration; NASA says it stabilizes Earth's wobble and has kept the climate less variable over thousands of years, so losing it threatens the steady tilt behind the seasons, not just the light.

Payoff: The Moon gave you tides that fed the coasts, a steady tilt that kept the seasons kind, and light on dark nights, and only once it is gone do you see that it was part of the machine keeping your world working.

Comment question (s10, exact text): If you could keep just one thing the Moon gives you, the tides, the steady seasons, or the light on a dark night, which one would you save, and why?

## s02

## s03

## s04

## s05

## s06

## s07

Over long years that slow drift turns into something you can feel, because the lean of the Earth sets your seasons, and if the lean wanders the seasons wander with it, so a place that grew wheat for a hundred summers may find its summers shorter and colder while rain falls where drought used to rule, and the change slides season by season and harvest by harvest until your grandchildren live in a world whose weather your grandparents would not recognize.

## s08

## s09

Humans adapt, because that is what humans do, as cities switch on brighter lights and sailors learn to navigate by stars and screens and poets search for a new symbol for longing, and children born after the disappearance hear stories about a silver face and ask if it was real, and festivals move to fixed dates that feel hollow, and you understand at last that the Moon was never decoration but part of the machine that kept your world working.
