By midnight, the animals notice what you cannot. Owls hunt in a dark so thick that their yellow eyes seem to float, and moths spiral around porch lights because the pale guide they have followed for millions of nights has vanished. Sea turtles hatch on a beach and crawl toward a horizon that offers no silver path, and they turn in confused circles on the sand while waves whisper lower than before. Bats still fly, and foxes still prowl, but the night has lost its lamp, and every creature that uses moonlight as a clock or a compass is suddenly guessing. You watch a moth bump against your window and you feel a small, cold thought land: if the animals are lost after one night without the Moon, what happens to the rest of the living world when the dark keeps coming, night after night, with no moonrise to end it?
